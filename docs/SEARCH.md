# Current bot planning and evidence boundaries

Rules v4 replaced the old population planner. The implementation is `engine/src/tactics/planner.rs`. Reports from the former `search.rs`, `balance` and `search_audit` examples describe archived rules and are not evidence for this design.

All difficulties share starting resources, production, cooldowns, costs and decision opportunities. They receive an observation-filtered copy before planning: concealed units and undiscovered sites are removed. A regression changes the hidden world and checks that the resulting order is unchanged.

The current controller plans settlement, production, research prerequisites, defended capitals, explicit attacks, counter recruitment, paid refits, retreats, naval approaches and orbital occupancy. Easy evaluates immediate exchanges. Medium also examines plausible adjacent enemy replies. Hard additionally values support, retreat routes, deployment and spacing against splash. Reply forecasts treat alternative enemy moves as alternatives, avoiding double-counted damage. Purchases consider range, visible clustering, access from the ocean and existing composition. This is a bounded positional forecast with strategic heuristics, not a general full-game minimax tree or a chess-strength proof. Prediction is limited by fog and eight independent opponents.

`pieces_audit` runs full matches with rotated player slots and fixed seeds. Its mixed mode tests guard, cavalry, archer, artillery, naval and space preferences using the same authoritative rules and action timing. Fixed-policy research is restricted to prerequisites relevant to that preference, so the audit does not deliberately drain their treasury on unrelated upgrades. The depth mode rotates a hard and easy player against six medium players. Both measure outcomes rather than assuming deeper forecasting always wins. Every controller gets one decision opportunity every two seconds, matching the shared order limit. An occasional opportunity is reserved for development, preventing an endless supply of available combat orders from starving research and production.

Finite tests cannot establish that no dominant policy exists. Map geography, discoveries, alliances and player choices can defeat a stronger planner. See `BALANCE.md` for the actual current-run results, including unfavorable results. Human playtesting remains necessary for exploit discovery, subjective interest, composition diversity and input burden.
