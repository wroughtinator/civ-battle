# Current bot planning and evidence boundaries

The current conquest/space rules build on the discrete-piece planner. The implementation is `engine/src/tactics/planner.rs`. Reports from the former `search.rs`, `balance` and `search_audit` examples describe archived rules and are not evidence for this design.

All difficulties share starting resources, production, cooldowns, costs and decision opportunities. They receive an observation-filtered copy before planning: concealed units and undiscovered sites are removed. A regression changes the hidden world and checks that the resulting order is unchanged.

The current controller plans settlement, production, research prerequisites, defended capitals, persistent attack focus, counter recruitment, retreats, naval approaches and orbital occupancy. Easy evaluates immediate exchanges. Medium also examines plausible adjacent enemy replies. Hard additionally values support, retreat routes, deployment and spacing against splash. Reply forecasts treat alternative enemy moves as alternatives, avoiding double-counted damage. Purchases consider range, visible clustering, access from the ocean and existing composition. This is a bounded positional forecast with strategic heuristics, not a general full-game minimax tree or a chess-strength proof. Prediction is limited by fog and eight independent opponents.

`pieces_audit` runs full matches with rotated player slots and fixed seeds. Its mixed mode tests guard, cavalry, archer, artillery, naval and space preferences using the same authoritative rules and action timing. Fixed policies initially prefer their own branch, then target orbital research after 6:00 so each can pursue space. The space policy targets the orbital prerequisite path from the start. The depth mode rotates a hard and easy player against six medium players. Both measure outcomes rather than assuming deeper forecasting always wins. Ordinary bots attempt decisions every two seconds, with no global command cooldown. The audit also measures slower eight-second controllers. An occasional opportunity is reserved for development, preventing an endless supply of available combat orders from starving research and production.

Finite tests cannot establish that no dominant policy exists. Map geography, discoveries, alliances and player choices can defeat a stronger planner. See `THREE_ERAS.md` and `PLANNING_AUDIT.md` for current focused planning results and the full-game audit; `STRATEGY_REDESIGN.md` preserves the earlier results. Human playtesting remains necessary for exploit discovery, subjective interest, composition diversity and input burden.

`npm run analyze:planning` uses a separate offline simulation controller to isolate candidate breadth, tactical foresight, slower/deeper versus faster/shallow play, and peaceful orbital-path launch scheduling. It does not change the live bot difficulties. `npm run analyze:design` retains the broader full-match, policy diversity, consequence and input-restraint gates. Both default to 120 seconds of analysis; compilation is separate.

The three-era roster adds situational directional walls, shield trucks, automatic healing and repair, anti-air and radar responses. Naval transport planning now checks for a reachable enemy coastline before purchasing a transport. The expansion policy aims for four cities instead of the other policies’ two. Settlers reserve founding funds upon reaching a legal site. Military policies pause pending research when an enemy civilization threatens a city and the army and treasury are low; the space policy deliberately accepts that risk to keep developing. Recruitment counts in every audit expose uncovered unit types. These policies remain limited tactical heuristics; coverage is not evidence that they use every unit optimally.
