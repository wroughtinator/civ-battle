## Audio from the owner's existing 99Sounds library

The audio is **not CC0**. It is incorporated under the [99Sounds license](https://99sounds.org/license/), which permits modification and incorporation into personal and commercial games. Standalone resale or redistribution of the sound library is prohibited. Do not distribute this project's audio as a sound pack or relabel it as CC0.

The source library was found at `C:/Users/camer/OneDrive/Documents/99sounds`. The source-file mapping is `audio-sources.json`; those complete WAV libraries are not shipped. `scripts/build-audio.py` extracts short sections, normalizes their loudness, crossfades ambience boundaries and encodes a combined 32 kHz mono 80 kbps MP3 atlas. The atlas and cue offsets are game runtime assets, without a download/catalog UI. The game loads this audio after user interaction.

Sources include 99 Sound Effects by Tomislav Zlatic (UI, orders and impacts), Water Sounds by Régis Royer (ocean surf), Nature Sounds by Free To Use Sounds (wind/vegetation), and Rain And Thunder (rain). Source attribution is retained here and in the file mapping. See the individual libraries at https://99sounds.org/ for their creators and license context.

## Rebuilding models

`export-models.py` runs inside Blender in background, factory-startup, disable-autoexec mode on the downloaded source files. The game does not ship Blender files, Blender Python code, a glTF loader, or Blender itself. Original meshes are rebuilt with `node scripts/build-original-models.mjs`. Downloaded source archives remain in local `artifacts`, outside the deployed public directory.

## Discrete-piece redesign (rules v4)

`guard.mesh`, `archer.mesh`, `scout.mesh`, `engineer.mesh`, `cavalry.mesh`, `artillery.mesh`, `settler.mesh`, `carrier.mesh` and `launcher.mesh` are original CC0 geometry generated in `scripts/build-original-models.mjs`. Together they add about 127 KB of uncompressed vertex data. The horse, wagon, bows, spears, wheels, carrier flight deck, aircraft and launcher silhouettes use actual triangles, surface normals and material colors. Walking, recoil, ocean bobbing, sonar rings, diving, founding progress and impact effects are rendered in the custom WebGL2 renderer. These are stylized models, not photorealistic scans.

All ten discovery props and the new settler, bow, cavalry, bombing, submerge, disband and territory SVG icons are original CC0 visual work. No image-generation or external icon service is needed. The prior CC0 ocean maps and licensed 99Sounds audio remain in use; their license terms are unchanged.

## Authored characters and woodland graphics pass

The current renderer replaces Guard, Archer and Scout with Quaternius' **Warrior, Ranger and Rogue** from [LowPoly RPG Characters](https://opengameart.org/content/lowpoly-rpg-characters), [source archive](https://opengameart.org/sites/default/files/rpg_characters_-_nov_2020.zip). The author's included `License.txt` dedicates the pack to **CC0 1.0**. Source/license checked 2026-09-17. Runtime: `guard.rig` (2,967 triangles), `archer.rig` (2,087), `scout.rig` (1,272).

`scripts/export-rigged.py` runs inside Blender 5.2 with `--background --factory-startup --disable-autoexec`, passing the corresponding source `.blend`, then `--python scripts/export-rigged.py -- guard` (or `archer` / `scout`). It reduces source meshes to 55%, samples their painted textures into vertex colors, preserves four normalized bone weights and the 32-bone skin, and samples only idle, run and weapon-specific attack clips at 20 Hz. Bone-parented weapons and accessories follow the author's rig. Guard uses Sword_Attack, Archer Bow_Attack_Shoot, Scout Dagger_Attack. The custom WebGL2 shader skins vertices on the GPU; poses interpolate with 80 ms transitions. No Blender files, source textures, glTF loader or animation framework ship to the browser. Original `.mesh` versions remain as reproducible historical assets but are no longer requested by the current renderer.

`woodland.mesh` and `woodland.webp` derive from carlosjorgereis' [Very low poly and texture tree](https://opengameart.org/content/very-low-poly-and-texture-tree), [tree01.zip](https://opengameart.org/sites/default/files/tree01.zip), **CC0 1.0** as stated on the author's page. `python scripts/export-tree.py <extracted directory>` converts the authored trunk, branches and leaf cards (661 triangles) and packs the two 256px color textures into a 512x256 WebP atlas. One geometry buffer is instanced with seeded rotation, height, crown proportions and color variation, mild branch sway, hemisphere culling and distant thinning. Beaches and tile centers remain clear of trunks. Trees are realistic textured foliage, not photogrammetry; variants share the same authored tree structure. The source archive and conversion intermediates stay in ignored `artifacts/graphics`.

Character meshes load on demand when their unit kind appears. Three rigs together are approximately 1.07 MB raw / 280 KB locally Brotli-compressed; the tree mesh and texture add approximately 78 KB raw. Exact current figures and caveats are in `size-report.json`. The renderer draws the visible forest in one instanced call. Local desktop browser review reported 60 fps and no WebGL errors; this is not a physical mobile GPU benchmark.

Every land/ocean edge receives a ground-following sand band, with shared corner distances and a wet-to-dry beach slope. Mountain geometry uses connected ridges with slope-sensitive rock shading and altitude/slope-limited snow. Ocean normal maps use wider, slower, lower-amplitude ripples. These terrain/shader changes remain original CC0 visual work.

Unit scales are in `public/combat-visuals.js`. Weapon cues last 260–650 ms: sword slash, cavalry charge, arrow, tank shell, artillery arc, commando burst, drone bomb, fleet broadside, submarine torpedo wake, carrier aircraft sortie, engineer pulse, launcher rocket, scout stab and settler bash. Actual attack eligibility stays authoritative (noncombat units do not gain attacks). Short cues occur at the end of existing windup; damage timing and recovery are unchanged. Impact flashes last half a second. `scripts/graphics-preview.mjs` is a local-only review fixture with synthetic events; `scripts/tactics-preview.mjs` exercises the actual command path. Neither fixture is deployed.
